A Kestrel measures the wind **at your position**. The bullet flies 500–1000 metres through terrain where the Kestrel is not. That is the fundamental limit of the tool.

## 1. Mirage

Focus the spotting scope slightly in front of the target. The tilt of the shimmer gives you direction; its speed gives you rough strength.

- Vertical, "boiling" mirage: almost no wind, or wind straight down the range.
- A 45° lean: roughly 3–4 m/s.
- Running flat and horizontal: 6 m/s or more.

Mirage stops being useful above about 8 m/s: it simply flattens out.

## 2. Vegetation

Dry grass starts to lean at 3 m/s. Bush branches move at 5–6 m/s. Tree trunks sway at 10 m/s. It is coarse, but it is available along the whole flight path.

## 3. Flags

If the range has flags, read the **farthest** one, not the one beside you. The bullet spends most of its flight time in the second half of the range, but takes most of its deflection in the first. Both halves have to be counted.

## 4. Dust and water

Dust lifted by an impact shows you the wind at the target itself. It is the most valuable indicator available and the most often ignored.

## How to train it

At every practice session, write down your wind call **before** you shoot, then write down the correction that actually worked. Two weeks of that will expose your systematic error. That is worth more than any device.
