When a new shooter turns up at the federation, the first piece of advice is almost always the same: start on .22LR. This is not about budget. It is about the rate of learning.

## The mistake is visible immediately

At 100 metres a .22LR bullet flies for about 0.35 seconds and drifts about as much as a 6.5 Creedmoor does at 600. The difference is that you see the impact through the scope, without losing the picture to recoil.

Seeing your own miss is exactly the property that teaches wind fastest.

## Recoil does not hide your errors

On a centerfire rifle a poor position is partly masked by recoil: you never see how you moved the muzzle. On .22LR every movement shows up on the target.

## The cost

Fifty rounds of .22LR costs roughly one round of 6.5 Creedmoor. Two hundred rounds in a session is realistic; the same on centerfire means a meaningful slice of barrel life and a monthly budget.

## What transfers

- Position and support building: **completely**
- Wind reading: **completely**
- Trigger control and breathing: **completely**
- Recoil management: **no**, that has to be learned separately

Three out of four transfer. That is a good enough trade.

## Getting started

Come to any rimfire practice. Rifle and ammunition are provided, free for your first two sessions.
